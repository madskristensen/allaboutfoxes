01 / FAMILY
✶

A dog cousin,
with a twist.

Foxes belong to Canidae, the family that also includes wolves and coyotes. Red foxes (Vulpes vulpes) and Arctic foxes (Vulpes lagopus) share a genus, Vulpes: a more specific branch of that family tree.

Why it matters: a silver fox is a color form of the red fox, not a separate species. Species names tell us more about relationships than coat color does. Compare the two species in Meet the Foxes.

02 / SUPER SENSES
◎

Those ears aren't
just for looks.

A red fox hunting a vole may freeze, aim its ears, then leap and bring its front feet down onto prey hidden in grass or snow. That high, arcing hunting pounce is often called mousing, even when the prey is not a mouse.

Why it matters: the fox can hunt without a clear view of its meal. In Yellowstone, voles, mice, and rabbits are among its prey; fruit and carrion broaden the menu. A fox is an opportunistic omnivore, not a hunter of just one food. Follow the hunt.

03 / THE FLUFFY STUFF
∿

A built-in
cozy blanket.

A resting fox can tuck its muzzle beneath its tail. Fur traps insulating air: it slows heat loss rather than making heat itself. Arctic foxes take insulation further with dense winter fur, including fur on the soles of their paws.

Why it matters: small rounded ears and a compact body expose less surface for heat to escape than long ears and limbs would. White-morph Arctic foxes also change from brown-gray summer fur to white winter fur; blue-morph foxes stay dark. Unpack the Arctic toolkit.

A question for curious minds: are all foxes red?

No! A red fox can be rusty, silver, or cross-colored. An Arctic fox can have a white or blue color morph, and both morphs molt seasonally. A blue morph does not turn snow-white in winter. Color is one clue; ear shape, body proportions, location, and season help complete the identification.

Research trail: Animal Diversity Web's red fox account, Yellowstone's red fox field notes, Wildlife Online's hunting research, and Alaska Department of Fish and Game's Arctic fox account.
