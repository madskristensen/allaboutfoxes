Some foxes pad across snowy ground. Others listen for tiny footsteps in a
desert. Foxes belong to the dog family, **Canidae**, but each species has its
own ways of finding food, staying comfortable, and raising a family.

Meet three members of the genus **Vulpes**: the adaptable **red fox**, the
tundra-specialist **Arctic fox**, and the tiny desert **fennec fox**. Discover
the tree-climbing **gray fox** and its smaller relative, the **island fox**,
in **Urocyon**. Then investigate the insect-hunting **bat-eared fox**, in
**Otocyon**. A genus groups closely related species within a family. These
foxes share ancestry, but not the same survival toolkit.

Compare the red fox's tall ears and dark lower legs with the Arctic fox's
rounded ears and compact build. Contrast two big-eared hunters, or compare
the mainland gray fox with its island relative. A silver fox is still a red
fox, a gray fox is a separate
species, and a summer Arctic fox need not be white. Each profile has
credited photographs, labeled field marks, a den cutaway, measurements with
context, and wildlife sources for checking the details yourself.
